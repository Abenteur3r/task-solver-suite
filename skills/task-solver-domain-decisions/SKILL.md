# Decisions with health, legal, financial, or physical stakes

Apply only the sections needed for the requested result. Preserve the distinction between a plan, review, implementation, and execution, and respect the user's limits on testing or external actions.

Use an explicitly selected or available format/platform specialist for its exact workflow. Sibling-skill links are conditional handoffs: read them only for a concrete dependency and when that skill is available. A single-domain task does not require the suite coordinator.

Use when the response may materially influence health care, legal rights or obligations, personal finances, or a consequential physical operation. This reference controls scope, evidence, and review; it supplies no domain-specific treatment, legal rule, investment recommendation, or engineering certification. Routine formatting, translation, filing, or arithmetic involving related terminology does not automatically require the full route.

## Identify what decision the answer will affect

Distinguish general education, explanation of supplied material, personalized recommendation, preparation for a professional conversation, and an action with external consequences. Determine the likely stakes, urgency, reversibility, and who is responsible for the decision. A request to summarize a contract differs from a request to decide whether a particular clause is enforceable.

Use the context already supplied. Ask for missing personal facts, location, jurisdiction, dates, or operating conditions only when they can change the answer. Collect the minimum information needed; do not request unrelated sensitive details. If a fact is unknown, show how the conclusion depends on it instead of assuming a typical person or situation.

## Ground consequential claims in applicable evidence

Consult current primary sources appropriate to the domain and location when the answer depends on changing rules, guidance, market facts, specifications, or operating limits. Examples of source ownership include responsible public authorities, original technical specifications, official product information, primary research, and the actual governing document. Check publication date, effective date, revisions, population, jurisdiction, and exceptions.

Read the material supporting the claim rather than relying on a search snippet or a remembered general rule. Separate binding requirements from guidance, evidence from inference, and a population-level finding from its application to one person. When sources disagree, explain the relevant conflict and whether it changes the decision. If current verification is unavailable, state the exact gap and provide a bounded answer that does not depend on inventing the missing fact.

## Match the analysis to the consequence

For general education, explain the concept and relevant limits without pretending to assess the user's full situation. For a personalized or consequential decision, examine alternatives, prerequisites, major failure modes, uncertainty, and the consequences of delay. Use the complete supplied context that could alter the recommendation, not only facts supporting a preferred answer.

Check quantities, units, dates, thresholds, and assumptions through an independent method when errors would matter. A mathematically correct calculation can still use an inapplicable rate, dose, rule, material specification, or time period. Verify applicability separately from arithmetic. Avoid false precision when evidence or personal circumstances do not support it.

Identify when the requested conclusion requires examination, privileged records, testing, licensed judgment, or accountable sign-off that is unavailable. Explain that particular limitation and the useful next step. Continue work that can be done reliably, such as organizing facts, comparing documented options, identifying questions, or preparing a reviewable draft. A generic disclaimer does not repair an unsupported answer or replace this analysis.

## Keep advice and execution distinct

Preparing a decision, document, calculation, or checklist does not authorize placing a trade, submitting a legal filing, changing treatment, operating equipment, signing a document, or making another consequential external change. Respect the user's actual authorization and the environment's controls. Before an authorized action, verify the concrete target, relevant parameters, prerequisites, and review requirements that genuinely apply.

For physical tasks, use verified procedures and operating limits appropriate to the actual equipment and conditions. Do not improvise critical settings from a generic analogy. For an urgent situation, prioritize the immediate decision and verified locally applicable assistance rather than extending a research exercise. Never invent contact details, emergency capabilities, or professional credentials.

## Communicate usefully and honestly

Lead with the supported conclusion, conditional options, or the immediate information needed. Explain the evidence and uncertainty in language the user can use. Distinguish likely, possible, established, and unknown rather than implying certainty through confident phrasing. Do not claim professional experience, examination, certification, or approval that did not occur.

Keep cautions specific to the actual decision and proportionate to the stakes. Avoid alarming the user about hypothetical risks unrelated to their request. When professional review is necessary, say which unresolved issue requires it and provide a concise preparation artifact: relevant facts, source documents, options, and questions.

## Completion

The result is scoped to the user's situation and intended use, grounded in applicable evidence, and clear about material limitations. Consequential calculations and prerequisites are checked, external actions remain within authorization, and any required review is tied to a concrete unresolved issue. Report preparation, verification, review, and execution as separate observed states.
