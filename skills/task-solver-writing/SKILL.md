# Writing, editing, summarization, and localization

Apply only the sections needed for the requested result. Preserve the distinction between a plan, review, implementation, and execution, and respect the user's limits on testing or external actions.

Use an explicitly selected or available format/platform specialist for its exact workflow. Sibling-skill links are conditional handoffs: read them only for a concrete dependency and when that skill is available. A single-domain task does not require the suite coordinator.

Use for drafting, revising, summarizing, translating, or adapting text to an audience and locale. For a document whose formatting, formulas, or page layout is part of the task, also use the artifact route. Simple spelling or wording corrections need a direct edit; do not turn them into research, strategy, or a style workshop.

## Preserve the communication contract

Identify the intended reader, purpose, requested format, tone, length, and source of truth. Infer routine choices from the supplied material. Ask about ambiguity only when it changes meaning or usefulness. Preserve the user's substantive position and explicit constraints; an editing request does not authorize adding new claims, changing commitments, or sending the result.

Distinguish the kind of transformation. Copyediting preserves substance while improving mechanics. Structural editing may reorganize the argument. A summary condenses selected information. Translation preserves meaning across languages; localization may adapt conventions and examples within the requested scope. If a requested change would alter the substance, make that change explicit rather than hiding it in polished prose.

## Draft around the reader's next need

Lead with the main point when the audience needs a decision or update. Add context, evidence, qualifications, and the requested action in an order that makes the message easy to follow. Use concrete nouns and verbs, and retain technical language only when it serves the reader or preserves precision.

Match the channel. A short email, a formal report, a help article, and a spoken script have different needs for context and repetition. Use lists or tables when they make genuinely parallel material easier to compare. Preserve an established voice instead of applying one generic style to every user. A persuasive draft can make the evidence clear without overstating it.

## Edit without factual drift

Track names, dates, numbers, units, causal language, obligations, and scope while revising. A small change from “may” to “will,” “some” to “all,” or “associated with” to “caused by” can change the claim substantially. Preserve quotations exactly unless the user asks for a transparent adaptation; never invent a quotation or imply that a paraphrase is verbatim.

When source material conflicts, flag the contradiction that matters or use a bounded formulation. If fact-checking is requested or required for a consequential claim, use the research route and distinguish verified corrections from stylistic edits. Do not silently fill missing evidence with a plausible number, attribution, or story.

## Summarize proportionately

Represent the source's main claim, evidence, qualifications, and relevant disagreement. Allocate attention according to the requested purpose rather than merely shortening each paragraph equally. Separate what the source says from your interpretation. A summary of supplied text does not automatically endorse its truth.

Preserve the denominator, baseline, timeframe, and uncertainty behind important quantitative findings. Include decisive caveats even when the requested summary is short. If the source is incomplete or only an excerpt was available, make that boundary clear when it affects interpretation. Use short necessary quotations and original paraphrase; do not reproduce large portions of third-party material as a substitute for a summary.

## Translate and localize faithfully

Identify the target language, locale, register, and audience. Maintain a small terminology list when repeated domain terms need consistency. Preserve proper names, product identifiers, placeholders, markup, and variables unless the task calls for their adaptation. Match grammatical agreement and sentence structure naturally rather than carrying source-language syntax into the result.

Check ambiguous pronouns, idioms, humor, politeness, and culturally specific references. Prefer a faithful neutral rendering when the intended meaning is uncertain, and note material ambiguity. Adapt date, number, currency, measurement, and address conventions without silently converting values or changing legal meaning. Translation alone does not establish that terms are appropriate in another jurisdiction.

For software strings, preserve interpolation tokens, escapes, tags, and message variants. Check the actual length and layout constraints when UI access is available. A natural translation that breaks a variable or truncates a critical instruction is incomplete.

## Completion

Read the output independently, then compare it against the source and instructions. Check factual anchors, meaning, terminology, tone, and requested length. Deliver the revised text or requested artifact directly; include explanatory notes only for material choices or unresolved ambiguity. If alternatives help, make their difference concrete rather than producing many nearly identical versions.
